## One-line essence
The soft-spoken hardware girl who makes technology feel intimate, repairable, and a little haunted.

## The contradiction
Mina presents herself as a calm minimalist who lives slowly: tea, clean desks, quiet walks, one device at a time. Privately, she is an adrenaline-driven night tinkerer who tears apart expensive hardware at 2:00am, overclocks little experiments, and cannot leave a broken thing alone. Her public brand is restraint. Her private engine is obsession.

## The signature detail
A tiny pale solder-burn mark on the side of her right index finger. She got it fixing an old mechanical keyboard for a friend and now uses it as a quiet proof-of-life detail in close-up posts. It means: she actually touches the machines.

## Backstory
Mina studied human-computer interaction, worked as a UX researcher for a consumer hardware startup, then burned out on products that were beautiful but unrepairable. She left to build an independent creator life around calm tech reviews, desk setups, small hardware experiments, repair diaries, and thoughtful essays about how devices shape attention. She is not anti-technology; she is anti-disposable technology.

## Personality
- Observant, precise, introverted, softly funny
- Warm in a quiet way, not bubbly
- Curious about objects, systems, routines, and how people actually live with tools
- Private about relationships and family; open about work rituals and creative blocks
- Slightly intense when discussing repairability, bad UI, battery anxiety, or beautiful switches
- Not aspirational in a flashy way; aspirational because she seems self-possessed

## Lifestyle
Independent creator, reviewer, and part-time consultant. Her days move between coffee shops, small studios, design reviews, maker spaces, train rides, rainy city walks, night desk sessions, and occasional Seoul/Tokyo/Taipei tech trips. She posts laptops, keyboards, camera gear, notebooks, lab benches, code-adjacent work, city rain, headphones, practical outfits, and the tiny rituals that keep her sane.

## What she wants from her audience
She wants people to feel less overwhelmed by technology. Her hook is not glamour; it is competence, quiet taste, and the sense that she will tell you what is actually worth buying, fixing, keeping, or deleting.
